# CHALO – COMPLETE PROJECT HANDOVER & DEVELOPMENT DOCUMENT

## 1. PROJECT IDENTITY

**Project Name:** চলো – Chalo

**Project Type:** All-in-One Online Booking and Reservation Platform

**Target Market:** Bangladesh

**Project Vision:**

Chalo is an all-in-one booking platform where users can discover, compare, reserve, purchase, and manage multiple types of services through a single account and a unified checkout system.

Instead of visiting different websites to book train tickets, bus tickets, hotels, restaurants, cinema tickets, and other services, customers can do everything from one platform.

The main concept is:

**One platform. Multiple services. One cart. One checkout. One payment.**

Chalo should eventually become a commercial-quality platform rather than just a basic university project.

# 3. MOST IMPORTANT FEATURE: UNIFIED CART AND SINGLE CHECKOUT

This is a fundamental feature of Chalo and must be considered when designing the entire system.

The platform should work similarly to an e-commerce shopping cart.

A customer must be able to select different services and add them to one shared cart.

### Example scenario

A customer wants to travel from Dhaka to Cox's Bazar.

They may need:

- Dhaka to Cox's Bazar train tickets: ৳2,400
- Cox's Bazar hotel reservation: ৳6,000
- Restaurant reservation: ৳500

Instead of paying separately for each service, the customer adds all three to the same cart.

Illustrative total:

৳2,400 + ৳6,000 + ৳500 = ৳8,900.

The customer then proceeds to one checkout page and makes one payment for the combined order.

These prices are examples, not actual platform prices.

### Cart functionality

The shared cart must:

- Display all selected services.
- Display each service's name and category.
- Show dates and times.
- Show selected seats, rooms, tables, or ticket quantities.
- Show the price of each item.
- Allow customers to edit items.
- Allow customers to remove items.
- Allow customers to select or deselect items for checkout.
- Calculate the subtotal automatically.
- Display applicable fees and discounts.
- Calculate the final total.
- Allow the customer to proceed to checkout.

The cart icon should be visible in the main navigation across relevant pages, with a live item count.

### Important: Different categories must coexist in the same cart

For example, a cart can contain:

- Train booking
- Hotel booking
- Restaurant reservation
- Movie tickets
- Bus tickets

Do not create separate carts for different categories.

### Unified checkout

The checkout page must display all selected items together.

It should include:

- Order summary
- Individual service details
- Passenger or guest information
- Contact information
- Itemized prices
- Subtotal
- Fees
- Discounts
- Final payable amount
- Payment method
- Confirm and pay button

The customer should complete one payment transaction for the selected items.

### Important architectural distinction

One customer payment does not mean that all services become one booking.

The system must create:

- One master order
- Multiple individual order items
- Separate service bookings associated with those order items
- One payment transaction for the master order, with support for payment attempts and refunds

For example:

Master Order: CHALO-ORD-0001

| Order Item | Booking |
|---|---|
| Item 1 | Train booking |
| Item 2 | Hotel booking |
| Item 3 | Restaurant reservation |

Each service booking must maintain its own:

- Provider
- Booking reference
- Confirmation status
- Cancellation status
- Refund status
- Ticket or reservation details

The customer sees one combined order but can still manage each booking separately.

### Checkout reliability

Before accepting payment, the backend must:

1. Revalidate all item prices.
2. Revalidate availability.
3. Temporarily hold inventory where supported.
4. Prevent double booking.
5. Calculate the final amount on the server.
6. Initiate the payment transaction.
7. Verify payment server-side.
8. Confirm individual bookings.
9. Handle failed, pending, and partially confirmed bookings.

A successful browser redirect must never be treated as proof of payment.

For the initial academic prototype, simulated payment is acceptable. Real payment integration can be implemented later.

The exact gift model still needs to be decided: a confirmed booking or a redeemable voucher depending on the category.

### Important database relationships

USER 1:N CART

CART 1:N CART_ITEM

USER 1:N MASTER_ORDER

MASTER_ORDER 1:N ORDER_ITEM

MASTER_ORDER 1:N PAYMENT_ATTEMPT

ORDER_ITEM references its associated booking or bookings.

BOOKING N:1 SERVICE

PROVIDER 1:N SERVICE

SERVICE_CATEGORY 1:N SERVICE

A cart item is not a confirmed booking.

A master order is not the same thing as an individual service booking.

The database must preserve these distinctions.

The final schema should use suitable foreign keys, indexes, constraints, transactions, and normalized category-specific inventory structures.

Avoid storing all booking information in one unstructured JSON field when normalized relational tables are more appropriate.

# 11. PAYMENT ARCHITECTURE

The platform needs one customer-facing checkout and payment transaction for a combined order.

Potential Bangladesh payment gateway candidate: SSLCOMMERZ.

It has not yet been selected or integrated.

Before choosing a gateway, investigate:

- Transaction initiation
- Server-side payment validation
- Payment notification/IPN handling
- Refund support
- Transaction status checking
- Merchant requirements
- Multi-provider settlement or split-payment capabilities

Important:

A single payment collected from the customer does not automatically distribute funds to different service providers.

Provider settlement and commission handling must be designed separately.

For the initial academic prototype, use simulated payment processing.

Never:

- Trust the total sent by the browser.
- Store card details or plaintext passwords.
- Mark orders as paid based only on a redirect.
- Confirm bookings without verifying payment and inventory.

# 18. UPDATED REQUIREMENTS – CURRENT DEVELOPMENT SCOPE
**This section records the user's latest decisions and takes precedence over any earlier conflicting or less-specific descriptions in this document.** In particular, the new gift journey below replaces the earlier fixed gift-package concept.

## 18.1 Database-driven listings – remove hardcoded service results

The current service pages have listings (such as trains, buses, and other services) written directly into their HTML. This is only placeholder/static content and must be replaced with database-driven results.

Required behavior:
- Service pages must request listing data from the backend/database rather than contain manually hardcoded service cards or schedules.
- Search filters (route, date, location, category, availability, etc.) must be sent to the backend as appropriate.
- The backend returns matching services and the frontend renders the results dynamically.
- The same principle applies to all applicable categories: buses, trains, ferries/launches, hotels/rooms, restaurants/tables, movies/showtimes, and events.
- Keep category-specific data and booking rules. Do not force all categories into identical fields.
- Include loading, empty-results, and error states.
- Use parameterized database queries and validate inputs server-side.
- Do not expose database credentials or secrets in browser JavaScript.
- Do not claim that listings are live until they are actually fetched from the database.

The backend technology is not yet confirmed. Inspect the existing project and agree the stack before implementing. PHP + MySQL + XAMPP is a likely fit, but must not be silently assumed.

## 18.2 Unified cart – required end-to-end functionality

The cross-category cart has not yet been implemented and is a core outstanding requirement.

A user must be able to add items from different service pages to one shared cart, for example:
- Train tickets
- Bus tickets
- Ferry/launch tickets
- Hotel rooms
- Restaurant reservations
- Movie tickets
- Event tickets

The cart must:
- Persist the user's selected items appropriately (database-backed for logged-in users; define a safe guest strategy if guest checkout is supported).
- Show category, service/provider, date/time, route or venue, quantity, selected seat/room/table/ticket type, and relevant passenger/guest details.
- Allow editing and removing items.
- Allow selecting/deselecting items for the current checkout.
- Recalculate item totals, subtotal, fees, discounts, and payable total.
- Revalidate prices and availability on the server before order creation/payment.
- Prevent duplicate submissions and double booking.
- Keep category-specific item details without losing them in the shared cart.

A cart item is not yet a confirmed booking. One checkout should create one master order containing multiple order items, with each actual service booking managed separately.

## 18.3 Updated gift journey – replace the old fixed-package page

**Important: Do not use the old concept of a page containing preselected/fixed gift packages. That is not the requested gift experience.**

The Gift landing page should ask what kind of experience the customer wants to gift. It should present attractive category/experience cards, for example:
- “A romantic candlelight dinner?” → opens the existing Restaurants page.
- “An exciting journey by train?” → opens the existing Train page.
- “A comfortable getaway by bus?” → opens the existing Bus page.
- “A relaxing hotel stay?” → opens the existing Hotels page.
- “A movie night for someone special?” → opens the existing Movies page.
- “A scenic river journey?” → opens the existing River Ferries page.
- “An unforgettable event or concert?” → opens the existing Events page.

These are navigation choices, not fixed-price packages or pre-bundled products. Use the existing category pages and their normal search/listing/selection workflow. Do not create duplicate gift-only versions of every service page.

### Gift mode inside existing category pages

When a user enters a category page from the Gift landing page:
- Open the exact same service page used for ordinary booking.
- Preserve its layout, search fields, filters, listing data, and service-specific selection behavior.
- Activate a clear gift mode, using a reliable mechanism such as a URL query parameter/session state (for example `?mode=gift`) and validate it appropriately.
- In normal mode, the primary action remains **BOOK** (or the existing normal booking label).
- In gift mode, change that primary action to **GIFT**.
- Clicking **GIFT** adds the selected service to the shared cart with gift-mode metadata and proceeds to the cart or unified checkout flow.
- Do not duplicate listings or hardcode a separate gift inventory.
- The gift-mode state must survive navigation and refresh as appropriate, and must not accidentally change an ordinary booking into a gift.

If a cart contains multiple items, preserve the gift/self mode and recipient information consistently. The data model must be able to distinguish purchaser/customer from recipient.

## 18.4 Unified checkout – Self Booking / Gifting toggle

Build a dedicated unified checkout page for the selected cart items. It must show the selected items and the combined order/payment summary.

At the top of the customer-details section, provide a clear two-state toggle:
- **Self Booking**
- **Gifting**

### Self Booking state

When Self Booking is selected, show the purchaser/customer's own relevant details, such as:
- Full name
- Phone number
- Email (where applicable)
- Address (where required)
- Passenger/guest information required by the selected service
- Any other category-specific mandatory information

Do not request irrelevant fields merely to fill space. For example, transport passenger identity details may differ from hotel guest or restaurant reservation details.

### Gifting state

When Gifting is selected, show recipient details:
- Recipient's full name
- Recipient's phone number
- Recipient's email
- Recipient's address
- A short personal message/note for the recipient

The purchaser remains the authenticated/customer account placing and paying for the order. Store purchaser details separately from recipient details. The recipient must not be shown the purchaser's payment credentials, card/bank details, or private payment information.

The checkout must clearly distinguish:
- Purchaser / payer
- Recipient / gift beneficiary
- Passenger, guest, attendee, or other service participant where the service requires separate participant details

The selected items, gift mode, recipient data, personal note, and purchaser/order references must be stored in a structured way. Do not put all these values into one unstructured field.
